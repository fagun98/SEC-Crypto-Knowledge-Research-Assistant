# 🔐 Custody Intelligence Weekly
## The Regulatory Digest for Crypto Custodians
### Week of 2026-02-16 – 2026-02-22 | Issue #1

## 🔥 Top Stories

### 1. Trading & Markets staff: “2% haircut” treatment for proprietary payment stablecoins under BD net capital
**(Feb. 19, 2026 — SEC Commissioner Statement re: staff FAQ)**  
Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do

**FACTS (with citations)**
- SEC Division of Trading & Markets staff issued an FAQ on applying Exchange Act Rule 15c3-1 to “payment stablecoins,” and staff “would not object” if a broker-dealer applies a **2% haircut** to proprietary payment stablecoin positions when calculating net capital. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
- Commissioner Peirce praised the approach, noted some broker-dealers had proposed a **100% haircut**, and urged consideration of **amending Rule 15c3-1** to address payment stablecoins more formally; she also solicited market input. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
- The staff FAQ’s “payment stablecoin” definition differs **pre- vs. post-effective date of the GENIUS Act** and ties eligibility to issuer regulation, reserve composition, redemption/disclosure policies, and **monthly attestations**. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)

**ANALYSIS**
- A staff “non-objection” at **2%** materially reduces the capital penalty versus 100%, which can make regulated broker-dealer stablecoin usage (e.g., settlement rails around tokenized securities) more feasible—especially if firms were previously capital-constrained by conservative internal haircuts.
- The FAQ’s eligibility conditions (issuer/regulation/reserves/attestations) function like a practical “allow-list” concept: firms may treat stablecoins differently depending on whether they match the SEC staff’s definitional criteria.

**INFERENCE**
- Expect broker-dealers to push for (and the SEC to receive) significant comment input on: (i) what stablecoins qualify, (ii) what attestation standards are sufficient, and (iii) whether/how haircut treatment should vary by reserve asset and redemption mechanics.

**Implications**
- **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** Staff would not object to a **2% haircut** for proprietary positions in qualifying payment stablecoins for net capital. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** If you custody stablecoins (directly or via sub-custody) to support BD activity, expect heightened diligence requests around issuer status, reserves, redemption terms, and evidence of monthly attestations.
- **RIAs & investment funds:**
  - **FACTS:** The statement notes the 2% approach aligns conceptually with haircuts applied to money market funds. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Even though this is a BD net-capital topic, RIA clients will ask whether “regulated” stablecoins have improved institutional acceptance—raising expectations for documented custody, liquidity, and issuer diligence.
- **Banks/trust companies:**
  - **FACTS:** The FAQ definition references issuer regulation and (pre-GENIUS Act) contemplates certain regulated issuers (including trust/bank pathways as described). (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Banks/trusts that issue or custody stablecoins should anticipate counterparties mapping onboarding criteria to the FAQ’s definition (attestations/reserves/disclosure).
- **Crypto-native custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** The staff definition conditions eligibility on disclosure/redemption and monthly attestations. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Prepare to evidence operational controls and reporting pipelines that help regulated clients demonstrate “qualifying” stablecoin handling (segregation, reconciliation, and proof of issuer attestations).

### 2. SEC leadership previews broker-dealer custody rulemaking for **non-security** crypto assets (incl. payment stablecoins)
**(Feb. 18, 2026 — Speech at ETHDenver)**  
Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude

**FACTS (with citations)**
- Chairman Atkins and Commissioner Peirce previewed forthcoming SEC work including **rulemaking on custody of non-security crypto assets (including payment stablecoins) by broker-dealers**. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
- They also described planned outputs: a Commission framework for **investment-contract analysis**, consideration of an **innovation exemption** for limited tokenized-securities trading, **transfer agent modernization** to accommodate blockchain recordkeeping, and additional no-action letters/exemptive orders (including on wallets/UIs). (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
- The speech emphasizes an incremental approach, collaboration, and exploring “privacy-preserving technologies,” including embedding compliance into smart contracts. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)

**ANALYSIS**
- This is a direct “heads-up” that the SEC wants a **defined pathway** for broker-dealers to custody assets that are *not* securities—closing a long-standing gap where custody expectations were clearer for securities than for broader crypto activity.
- Wallet and user-interface relief (via no-action/exemptive tools) could materially change who can provide “custody-adjacent” services without triggering Exchange Act registration—depending on how the SEC draws functional lines (control, key possession, authorization, etc.).

**INFERENCE**
- The eventual BD custody framework for non-security crypto is likely to borrow concepts from customer protection norms (segregation, control environment, governance, auditability), while adapting them to key management and on-chain operational realities.

**Implications**
- **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** SEC leadership explicitly signaled **upcoming rulemaking** on BD custody of non-security crypto assets. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Start mapping your current wallet/key architecture to potential supervisory expectations (authorization, approvals, independent checks, incident response, reconciliations).
- **RIAs & investment funds:**
  - **FACTS:** The SEC previewed additional guidance/no-action/exemptive actions touching wallets/UIs and tokenized securities. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** If RIAs rely on BD platforms or prime brokers for crypto exposure, custody rulemaking could shift what structures are permissible and what disclosures become “table stakes.”
- **Banks/trust companies:**
  - **FACTS:** Transfer agent modernization to accommodate blockchain recordkeeping is on the SEC’s forward agenda. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Banks/trusts that custody tokenized securities may need to integrate with new recordkeeping/TA models (and demonstrate how custody records reconcile to on-chain state).
- **Crypto-native custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** The SEC anticipates more no-action/exemptive outputs, including on wallets and user interfaces. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** This is a window to seek clarity/relief—especially for custody-adjacent products—before rules harden into prescriptive requirements.

### 3. SEC formalizes Crypto Task Force + expands SEC–CFTC harmonization channel (Project Crypto posture)
**(Feb. 16, 2026 — SEC pages)**  
Sources:  
- Crypto Task Force: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force  
- SEC–CFTC Harmonization Initiative: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative

**FACTS (with citations)**
- The SEC Crypto Task Force was formed to clarify how federal securities laws apply to crypto assets and recommend policy measures; it will engage through meetings, written input, roundtables, and information requests, and coordinate with other regulators. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)  
- Commissioner Hester M. Peirce is designated to lead the Crypto Task Force. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)  
- The SEC–CFTC Harmonization Initiative states it aims to coordinate and reduce duplicative regulation, clarify jurisdictional boundaries, and engage stakeholders via meetings/roundtables/written input, while preserving robust investor/market protections. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)

**ANALYSIS**
- For custody specifically, this setup increases the probability of clearer “who can hold what” lines—especially for hybrid models involving tokenized securities (SEC) plus spot commodities / derivatives (CFTC).
- The public-engagement emphasis is a signal that well-structured custody control proposals (segregation models, key management governance, audit/attestation, incident reporting) may meaningfully influence policy direction.

**INFERENCE**
- Expect the Task Force and harmonization channel to become the primary intake for market structure and custody proposals—potentially accelerating templates for compliant custody of tokenized securities and stablecoin settlement models.

**Implications**
- **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** The Task Force is explicitly focused on “realistic paths to registration for…market intermediaries.” (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** If you operate custody functions that resemble broker-dealer/control functions, anticipate more direct SEC engagement and potential pathway creation (or enforcement focus where firms remain outside).
- **RIAs & investment funds:**
  - **FACTS:** The Task Force aims to craft tailored disclosure frameworks and draw lines between securities and non-securities. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** RIA custody positioning often hinges on asset classification + “qualified custodian” posture; clearer lines may reduce ambiguity but raise baseline expectations for controls and disclosure.
- **Banks/trust companies:**
  - **FACTS:** The harmonization initiative emphasizes coordination and eliminating duplicative/conflicting requirements. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Banks/trusts could benefit from clearer, unified expectations—if they can translate bank-grade controls into crypto key-management and on-chain operational evidence.
- **Crypto-native custodians:**
  - **FACTS:** Both initiatives invite stakeholder engagement via meetings and written input. (Sources: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force; https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)  
  - **ANALYSIS:** Treat this as an “open docket without a docket number”: submit concrete custody-control proposals and seek clarity on custody-adjacent product boundaries (wallets/UIs, authorization, transaction governance).

## 💡 Compliance Action Items

- [ ] **Broker-dealers:** Recalculate net capital impacts of moving from internal 100% stablecoin haircuts toward the **2%** staff non-objection posture—*but only for stablecoins that match the staff “payment stablecoin” definition/criteria.* (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
- [ ] **All custodians (banks/trusts + crypto-native):** Build an “evidence packet” for stablecoin eligibility: issuer regulatory status, reserve composition, redemption policy disclosures, and monthly attestation availability (and how you ingest/monitor them). (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-stablecoin-021926-cutting-two-would-do)  
- [ ] **Custody product / legal:** Draft a short written-input outline for the SEC on (i) custody of non-security crypto assets by broker-dealers, and (ii) practical control standards for keys, approvals, and incident response. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-peirce-021826-number-go-down-other-schadenfreude)  
- [ ] **Funds + fund service providers:** If you support registered funds, gap-assess operational readiness for proposed Form N-PORT timing/data changes (45-day filing; quarterly public disclosure; ETF share-class identifiers). (Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/ic-35962.pdf)  
- [ ] **Onboarding/operations (all intermediaries):** Strengthen “authorized destination” controls (verified accounts/wallets, entity authority documents, dual-approval, and exception escalation), reflecting enforcement themes around unauthorized accounts and affiliate payments. (Sources: https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26486; https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26483)  
- [ ] **Counterparty diligence:** Validate adviser/fund counterparties’ core representations (e.g., filings/addresses/status) and retention of substantiating records—consistent with the SEC’s Form ADV misrepresentation enforcement posture. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26484)

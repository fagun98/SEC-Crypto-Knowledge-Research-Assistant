## 🔥 Top Stories

1. **SEC Chair spotlights “Project Crypto” (SEC–CFTC) for token taxonomy + possible on-chain exemptions** (Feb. 11–12, 2026 | SEC testimony)
   - **FACTS:** SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins testified that the SEC and CFTC have formed a joint effort (“Project Crypto”) to **consider a token taxonomy** and **potential exemptions to enable on-chain movement/transactions**. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-testimony-hfsc-021126; https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-testimony-sbhuac-021226)  
   - **FACTS:** The SEC frames the need as a missing federal framework—“A federal framework for crypto markets is long overdue.” (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-testimony-hfsc-021126; https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-testimony-sbhuac-021226)  
   - **FACTS:** The SEC also published a standalone SEC–CFTC “Harmonization Initiative” page describing goals to reduce duplicative rules, clarify jurisdictional boundaries, and create streamlined pathways for innovative products while preserving protections. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)  
   - **ANALYSIS:** For custody providers, a taxonomy is the “front door” to everything else: whether an asset is treated as a security can determine whether broker-dealer customer protection concepts, adviser custody expectations, and securities-style controls (segregation, books/records, examinations) become the baseline.  
   - **INFERENCE:** Expect industry pressure to translate “on-chain exemptions” into concrete conditions (e.g., control standards, audit trails, and who must intermediate transfers) rather than broad carve-outs—especially where retail customers are involved.

   **Implications (ANALYSIS):**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - Start mapping which tokens/products you touch could land on the “security” side of a future taxonomy, and pre-plan what it would take to custody/trade them under securities obligations (recordkeeping, surveillance, customer protection concepts).
     - Prepare for the possibility that any “on-chain exemption” will still require strong supervisory controls, especially around transaction authorization and audit trails.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - Review whether your current custody model can flex if some client crypto positions are more clearly characterized as securities (and therefore pull in stricter custody expectations and disclosure).
     - Update internal risk registers for “taxonomy reclassification” and downstream impacts (qualified custodian reliance, reporting, valuation governance).
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - Anticipate clearer (but potentially higher) expectations for banks/trusts offering crypto custody if a federal framework/taxonomy becomes more concrete; plan for examination-ready evidence of segregation, controls, and governance.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - Treat taxonomy work as a catalyst for tougher client due diligence and asset acceptance standards—especially if you custody tokens that could be treated as securities.
     - Engage early: harmonization + taxonomy discussions tend to “lock in” operational assumptions (what constitutes control, what records must exist, and who is accountable).

2. **Commissioner Uyeda: tokenization is “technology-neutral,” but tokenized securities remain subject to securities regulation** (Feb. 9, 2026 | SEC speech)
   - **FACTS:** Commissioner Mark Uyeda stated the SEC’s approach to tokenization should be **technology-neutral and outcomes-focused**. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)  
   - **FACTS:** He also emphasized: **“Tokenized versions of securities remain subject to securities regulation.”** (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)  
   - **FACTS:** Uyeda described the SEC using **exemptive relief and scoped pilots** to permit controlled experiments, and noted a recently noticed **Investment Company Act exemptive application related to tokenized assets**. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)  
   - **ANALYSIS:** The SEC is implicitly telling custodians: “tokenization doesn’t change the compliance target.” If you custody tokenized securities, expect securities-grade custody expectations (controls, auditability, disclosures) regardless of whether the asset moves on-chain.
   - **INFERENCE:** Pilots/exemptive relief are likely to become the “practical path” for institutions that want to use blockchain rails while avoiding an all-or-nothing regulatory posture—meaning custody providers may need to operate in a world of bespoke conditions (reporting, transparency, and operational constraints) per pilot.

   **Implications (ANALYSIS):**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - If you plan to custody tokenized securities, assume the SEC will look for traditional protections “mapped” into token workflows (clear customer ownership records, operational control boundaries, robust reconciliation).
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - For tokenized securities exposure, increase due diligence on how custody is evidenced (on-chain records vs. internal ledgers), and whether the structure relies on exemptive relief/pilots with embedded conditions.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - Banks/trusts can position themselves as “safe custody” anchors in tokenization pilots—if they can demonstrate segregation, operational risk controls, and examination-ready reporting.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - Treat “technology-neutral” as “controls-neutral”: you’ll still need institution-grade key management, access governance, incident response, and auditable records if the underlying is a security.

3. **Enforcement drumbeat reinforces custody basics: misappropriation, opaque omnibus practices, and weak books/records** (Feb. 10–12, 2026 | SEC enforcement releases/orders)
   - **FACTS:** In *SEC v. John David Gessin, et al.*, the SEC obtained final default judgments after alleging investors’ funds were misappropriated for personal expenses; the court ordered permanent injunctions and disgorgement plus prejudgment interest. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26481)  
   - **FACTS:** The related final judgments expressly bind defendants and, in places, persons acting “in concert,” and contemplate SEC collection procedures and potential Fair Fund-style distribution mechanics. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/litreleases/2026/judg26481-johndavidgessin.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/litreleases/2026/judg26481-equifunds-inc.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/litreleases/judg26481-icefleetllc.pdf)  
   - **FACTS:** In the Barrington administrative order, the SEC found an adviser CCO used omnibus trading accounts and allocated profitable day trades to himself while allocating losing trades to clients, and that Form ADV representations about review controls were not matched by actual review. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2026/34-104793.pdf)  
   - **FACTS:** A jury found a CryptoFX salesperson liable in a case where the SEC described CryptoFX as a Ponzi scheme that promised crypto/FX trading but used funds for Ponzi payments, commissions, and personal expenses. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/ryan-statement-ismael-sanchez-021226)  
   - **ANALYSIS:** Even when the week’s “big policy” theme is token taxonomy, the SEC’s enforcement messaging is still anchored to custody primitives: don’t commingle/misuse client assets, don’t misstate controls, and don’t run custody operations you can’t evidence with records.  
   - **INFERENCE:** As token taxonomy and tokenized securities expand, the SEC is likely to apply these same theories (misappropriation, misleading statements about controls, and books-and-records failures) to digital-asset custody settings—especially where pooled/omnibus structures blur client entitlements.

   **Implications (ANALYSIS):**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - Tighten surveillance and documentation around omnibus workflows, allocation logic, and any employee/affiliate activity that could create “preferencing” or misappropriation optics.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - If you rely on pooled execution or omnibus arrangements (including via crypto platforms), demand audit-ready allocation evidence and ensure Form ADV/control disclosures match reality.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - Reassess onboarding/monitoring to detect misuse of customer assets and ensure you can support legal process quickly (freezes, remittances, distributions) where enforcement matters touch assets you hold.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - Assume enforcement will test: (1) segregation truthfulness, (2) who can move assets and why, and (3) whether you can reconcile and prove client entitlements quickly under stress.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This SEC Crypto Task Force roundtable (May 12, 2025) convened Commissioners and a broad set of market participants, service‑providers and industry technologists to discuss tokenization of traditional securities — its opportunities, operational and legal challenges, and possible regulatory approaches. Key themes:
- Strong industry enthusiasm for tokenization’s potential to improve settlement, collateral utility, operational efficiency, fractional access, and new product structures (examples: tokenized money market products, tokenized yield funds).
- Commissioners emphasized policy clarity, technology‑neutral principles, and careful calibration: Commissioner Peirce and others advocated for sandboxes/exemptive relief and permissive approaches; Commissioner Crenshaw and others urged caution on system‑level risks (settlement, netting, market resilience) and asked “what problem are we solving?”
- Concrete near‑term regulatory items discussed: rescission of SAB 121 (staff action already taken was discussed as an enabling step), possible qualified‑custodian rule updates, transfer agent modernization, ATS/market structure modernization, and design of pilot/sandbox/conditional exemptive relief.
- Divergent views on public permissionless blockchains as market infrastructure: some speakers view them as necessary open infrastructure (and resilient); others raised operational and governance risks that may require regulation or avoidance for system‑critical use.
Overall outcome: The meeting advanced detailed, pragmatic discussion, produced explicit requests for staff work and public input (including the Crypto Task Force comment portal), and signaled the Commission will pursue a mix of staff guidance, rulemaking/exemptive relief, and pilot programs to test tokenization approaches.

3. VIEWS ON TOPICS
This section identifies the main topics discussed, summarizes differing viewpoints, and notes consensus or disagreements.

Topic A — Definition and scope of tokenization
- Viewpoints:
  - Many panelists defined tokenization as a “new file format” / digital wrapper for a security enabling registered ownership, programmability, and composability (e.g., Alex: “tokenization… is just a new file format”; Franklin Templeton: “a token is a wrapper.”) (Transcript).
  - Some emphasized the spectrum of tokenization — from on‑chain representation with off‑chain legal rights to fully native on‑chain enforcement (spectrum framework referenced).
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Consensus that tokenized securities are still securities under the securities laws when they represent the same economic and legal rights.
  - Disagreement about whether tokenization on public permissionless chains is appropriate for system‑critical securities infrastructure — some speakers promoted public chains and open infrastructure; others cautioned about operational/governance risks on permissionless chains.

Topic B — Issuance / Registration: regulatory treatment
- Viewpoints:
  - Many (e.g., Commissioner Peirce, BlackRock) urged “same security, same regulatory treatment” as a baseline: tokenization doesn’t change the substance of a security.
  - Some noted that existing registration forms (Form S‑1, etc.) are not tailored to crypto and that the Commission should explore adjusted forms/exemptions/safe harbors to facilitate registered tokenized offerings.
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Broadly agreed that legal clarity is needed; disagreement about the extent/pace of adaptation and whether new exemptions or guidance should be created immediately vs. after pilots.

Topic C — Custody and qualified custodian rules
- Viewpoints:
  - Chair Atkins cited the rescission of SAB 121 as an important step to remove staff‑level impediments and suggested revisiting who qualifies as a custodian and expanded optionality (Transcript; RAG: SAB 122 rescinded SAB 121).
  - Many industry participants asked for clarity on qualified custodian eligibility (including recognition of modernized custody and self‑custody models with protections).
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Consensus that custody rules need review and clarity. Disagreement on whether self‑custody should be broadly permitted, and on how to define “qualified custodian” for tokenized securities.

Topic D — Trading, market structure, and settlement cycles
- Viewpoints:
  - Tokenization proponents argued tokenization enables faster settlement, programmable settlement (T+0 options), better collateral utility, 24/7 transferability, and new market primitives (AMMs, improved liquidity, fractionalization).
  - Cautioning voices (Commissioner Crenshaw, DTCC, others) emphasized netting benefits, liquidity preservation, and that instant settlement may remove important protections (pause, investigation) and could disadvantage retail investors.
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Consensus that trading rules/ATS/NMS considerations require review; disagreement on whether T+0 should be a universal goal or optionality for participants.

Topic E — Interoperability (technical and regulatory)
- Viewpoints:
  - Industry repeatedly stressed interoperability across chains, market systems, and legal regimes; DTCC and others urged standards/consensus.
  - Regulators and some industry participants emphasized regulatory interoperability (treating on‑chain/off‑chain equivalents consistently).
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Broad agreement that interoperability is essential and a major challenge. No consensus on how to achieve it; some favored industry‑led standards, others recommended regulatory guideposts.

Topic F — Operational risks, smart contracts, and permissionless public chains
- Viewpoints:
  - Supporters say public blockchains improve transparency, resilience to insider risk, and provide open, widely standardized infrastructure.
  - Critics raised multiple operational and governance risks (validator/operator behavior, secret emergency patches, MEV/externalization of payments, oracle risks, inability to “pause” transactions easily), and urged either avoiding permissionless public chains for system‑critical functions or imposing governance controls.
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Agreement that operational risks must be addressed; strong disagreement about whether permissionless chains can be used safely for securities record‑keeping and whether the SEC should treat chain operators/validators as part of regulated infrastructure.

Topic G — Regulatory experimentation: sandboxes, conditional exemptive relief, pilots
- Viewpoints:
  - Many industry speakers strongly supported structured sandboxes/pilots/exemptive relief to enable onshore innovation and to test technical approaches (Commissioner Peirce championed a sandbox approach).
  - Some urged sandboxes to be purpose‑driven and designed to test regulatory rule adjustments (not just tech capability) and to be global in scope.
- Consensus/Disagreements:
  - Broad support for pilot programs; differences on design (scope, size, conditions, whether to call them “sandbox” vs. targeted relief).

4. MEETING CONCLUSION
Overall outcome
- No single rulemaking was adopted at the meeting, but the Commission signalled active next steps: staff were asked to consider guidance, exemptions and updates (issuance, custody, ATS modernization), and the community was invited to provide input via the Crypto Task Force webpage and to participate in pilots/sandboxes. Chair and Commissioners set policy priorities and urged coordinated staff work.

Action items / next steps (explicitly stated or inferred from transcript)
- SEC staff to continue providing clarifications and consider whether additional guidance, registration exemptions, or safe harbors are needed for crypto asset issuances (Chair Atkins: “I’ve asked the Commission staff to consider whether additional guidance, registration exemptions, and safe harbors are needed”). (Transcript)
- Rescission of SAB 121 was announced/recognized as completed (discussion in meeting); further work to define qualified custodian eligibility under Advisers Act and Investment Company Act (Chair Atkins).
  - Context: SAB 122 rescinded prior SAB 121 (SEC staff bulletin) — (RAG search result: Staff Accounting Bulletin No. 122 rescinds SAB 121) — link: https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/staff-accounting-bulletins/staff-accounting-bulletin-122.
- Staff to explore ATS modernization and whether guidance/rulemaking could facilitate listing and trading of crypto assets on national exchanges.
- Staff and industry to work on transfer agent rule modernization (tokenization/transfer agent or “tokenization agent” role), and clarifications that a public ledger could serve as a definitive record of ownership where appropriate (panelist proposals).
- SEC requested public input via Crypto Task Force portal and invited continued engagement; Commissioner Peirce specifically asked for feedback and announced additional roundtable (June 9) (Transcript).
- Industry pilots / sandbox design: multiple panelists requested clear, outcome‑oriented pilot programs that inform rule changes rather than purely experimental sandboxes.

Key takeaways
- Tokenization offers tangible operational and business benefits (real‑time accounting, programmable corporate actions, collateral mobility, fractionalization and broader investor access) demonstrated by practitioners (Franklin Templeton, BlackRock, DTCC experiments).
- Legal/regulatory uncertainty (custody rules, transfer agent treatment, ATS/NMS interactions, qualified custodian eligibility, and cross‑jurisdictional issues) remains the principal adoption bottleneck.
- The SEC signaled willingness to employ a combination of staff guidance, conditional exemptive relief/pilot programs, and rulemaking to create pathways for tokenization while preserving investor protection and market integrity.
- There are unresolved and material questions about the use of public permissionless blockchains for system‑critical functions; regulators and market infrastructure providers are asking for principled standards and interoperability work.

CONCLUDING OBSERVATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS (analyst judgment based on transcript)
1. The Commission is moving from enforcement‑led regulation to a proactive combination of guidance, pilots/exemptions, and targeted rulemaking. Market participants should expect a staged approach: immediate clarifications and staff guidance, followed by targeted pilot programs and then rulemaking where necessary.
2. Priority regulatory workstreams that emerged and deserve prompt attention:
   - Custody: clarify qualified custodian definitions and whether self‑custody can be an investor‑choice path with appropriate disclosure/safeguards.
   - Transfer agent modernization: accommodate tokenization (explicitly enable tokenization agents) and define responsibilities for wallet recovery, freezes, sanctions/compliance, corporate actions and issuer recordkeeping.
   - Market structure: assess ATS/NMS interactions with tokenized trading, define how best‑price obligations and trade‑through protections apply or are adapted for atomically settled trades.
   - Interoperability & security interest perfection: work with state commercial law actors and industry on standards to perfect security interests in tokenized collateral cross‑jurisdictionally.
   - Pilot/sandbox design: a clear, outcome‑oriented sandbox that tests both technical implementations and the practical operation of proposed rule changes is essential (industry asked that pilots be designed to produce evidence to inform rulemaking).
3. Operational governance thresholds: For systemically significant uses (e.g., national market infrastructure, central depositories), the SEC should define minimum resiliency and governance standards for any ledger or validator set (whether permissioned or permissionless). These can be implemented via guidance, staff interpretive letters, or exchanges/clearing agencies subject to oversight.
4. Continue public engagement: the Commission should ensure ample public comment windows, and require pilot participants to publish lessons learned (non‑confidential summaries) to accelerate consensus on taxonomy and interoperability standards.

## 🔥 Top Story

### No new SEC custody publications were provided for this week; focus remains on *standing* custody themes: safeguarding/qualified custodian standards, segregation, and cyber/key-management controls.

**FACTS (from provided SEC items):**
- The SEC has an active **proposed** rulemaking titled **“Safeguarding Advisory Client Assets (S7-04-23)”** under Investment Management, reopening a comment period (IA-6384), focused on safeguarding advisory client assets and advisers’ custodial arrangements.  
  Source: SEC rulemaking activity page ([link](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/rulemaking-activity?search=&rulemaking_status=178631&division_office=All&year=All))
- The SEC has an active **proposed** rulemaking titled **“Daily Computation of Customer and Broker-Dealer Reserve Requirements (S7-11-23)”** under Trading & Markets addressing **daily** reserve computations under the Customer Protection Rule (34-97877).  
  Source: SEC rulemaking activity page ([link](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/rulemaking-activity?search=&rulemaking_status=178631&division_office=All&year=All))
- The SEC cybersecurity page links to an investor bulletin **“Crypto Asset Custody Basics for Retail Investors,”** and states FY2026 exam priorities include policies/procedures, governance, data loss prevention, access controls, account management, and incident response (e.g., ransomware).  
  Source: SEC cybersecurity page ([link](https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity))

**ANALYSIS (implications for crypto custody programs):**
- Even absent new publications this week, the standing SEC posture reflected in the dataset is consistent: **custody = segregation + reliable books/records + operational control environment**, with **cybersecurity/key management** treated as inseparable from custody.
- For firms touching customer crypto (directly or via vendors), expect scrutiny to concentrate on **who qualifies as a custodian, how assets are segregated/controlled, and whether cyber controls are provable and testable**.

**INFERENCE (what to watch next):**
- The next SEC “move” most likely to change day-to-day custody operations would be progress on **finalizing or further reproposing** adviser safeguarding/qualified-custodian expectations or advancing broker-dealer reserve computation reforms—both would cascade into vendor oversight, reconciliations, and reporting SLAs.

## 💡 Compliance Action Items

1. **Map custody obligations to cyber controls (key management = custody control).**  
   - **FACTS:** SEC exam priorities (FY2026) include access controls, governance, and incident response; SEC content links to crypto custody basics. (sec-5, https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity)  
   - **ACTION:** Ensure you can produce evidence of privileged access reviews, key-management approvals, and incident runbooks/tabletop results.

2. **Pressure-test segregation and reconciliation SLAs with any custodian/vendor.**  
   - **FACTS:** SEC proposals emphasize segregation/accounting rigor (daily reserve computations for BDs; safeguarding advisory assets for RIAs). (sec-3, sec-1)  
   - **ACTION:** Confirm reconciliation frequency, cutoffs, exception handling, and independence (who can move assets vs who reconciles).

3. **RIA-specific: inventory all client asset types you “touch,” including crypto.**  
   - **FACTS:** Proposed safeguarding rule concerns “advisory client assets” and custodial arrangements. (sec-1)  
   - **ACTION:** Document which assets are held where, who is (and is not) a custodian, and what disclosures you provide about those arrangements.

4. **BD-specific: assess readiness for tighter reserve-computation cadence.**  
   - **FACTS:** SEC proposal contemplates daily reserve computations under the Customer Protection Rule. (sec-3)  
   - **ACTION:** Identify data dependencies (custodian feeds, valuations, settlement timing) that would break under daily computation expectations.

5. **Banks/trusts & crypto-native custodians: package “exam-ready” custody control evidence.**  
   - **FACTS:** SEC cyber focus emphasizes governance, access controls, and data loss prevention. (sec-5)  
   - **ACTION:** Maintain a standing evidence binder: SOC reports (if applicable), access control matrices, key-ceremony records, incident response testing, and segregation attestations.

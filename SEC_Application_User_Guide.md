# SEC Crypto Application — One-Page User Guide

**A short guide for business users**

---

## What This Tool Does

The SEC Crypto Application helps you stay on top of U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) rules, guidance, and developments that affect crypto and custody. It gives you:

- **Answers to SEC-related questions** (in plain English, with source links)
- **A weekly custody newsletter** (summary of the past week’s SEC developments)
- **Round table meeting reports** (structured summaries of SEC round table meetings)
- **An overall regulatory report** (combined view across multiple round tables)

Everything is based on **official SEC and related credible sources**—not general web content or unsourced AI.

---

## What Problems It Solves

| Problem | How the Tool Helps |
|--------|---------------------|
| Keeping up with SEC crypto and custody updates | Weekly newsletter and one-place summaries so you don’t have to scan SEC.gov yourself. |
| Needing accurate, traceable answers for compliance or policy | AI assistant and reports cite SEC sources; you can open the original document with one click. |
| Understanding what was said at SEC round tables | Structured meeting reports (speakers, topics, conclusions) and an overall report across meetings. |
| Avoiding unsourced or speculative answers | All answers and reports are grounded in the application’s SEC knowledge base and cited sources. |

---

## How to Use Each Function

1. **SEC AI Assistant (Q&A)**  
   Type your question in plain English (e.g. “How does the SEC treat stablecoins?”). You get an answer plus a **Sources** section with links to SEC.gov. Use it for quick checks, compliance research, or briefing prep.

2. **Custody Newsletter**  
   A weekly report is generated (e.g. every Monday for the prior week). Read **This Week at a Glance**, then **Top Stories** and **Compliance Action Items**. Use **Resource Library** links to open any source on SEC.gov.

3. **Round Table Meeting Report**  
   Provide the meeting URL and transcript. The tool produces a report with Executive Summary, Key Speakers, Views on Topics, Conclusion, and Key Points. Use it for internal briefings and to compare meetings over time.

4. **Overall Round Table Report**  
   Run this after you have two or more round table reports. You get one document with an Executive Brief, cross-meeting themes, speaker and topic analysis, “What This Means For…” by stakeholder, and future predictions (with clear labels). Use it for leadership and strategy.

---

## What Makes It Different From Generic AI Tools

- **SEC-only focus:** Built to answer from a structured **SEC knowledge base**, not the open internet. It does not mix in random or unverified sources.
- **Source transparency:** Every answer and report includes **direct links** to SEC.gov (or other configured official sources). You can verify and go deeper yourself.
- **Structured outputs:** Newsletters and reports follow fixed sections (e.g. FACTS vs ANALYSIS, implications by role, Compliance Action Items), so you always know where to look.
- **Designed for compliance and policy:** Outputs are suitable for internal use, briefings, and audit trails because they are tied to cited, official material.

---

## How Outputs Are Grounded in SEC Sources

- **AI Assistant:** Your question is turned into search topics, and the tool **retrieves only** content from the SEC knowledge base (indexed SEC.gov documents). The answer is written from those retrieved passages and ends with a **Sources** list (SEC.gov URLs).
- **Newsletter:** Content is **discovered and extracted** from configured SEC.gov pages (speeches, task force, enforcement, rulemaking) for the chosen week. Every fact is tagged (FACTS/ANALYSIS/INFERENCE) and linked to a source URL; the **Resource Library** table lists all sources.
- **Round Table and Overall Reports:** Meeting reports use the transcript and optional **SEC knowledge base (RAG)** for context. The overall report **aggregates only** from existing round table reports and optional RAG/web context. All reports include a **Sources** section.

**In short:** If you see a claim in an answer or report, it is either from the SEC knowledge base, from an SEC document linked in the report, or from the meeting transcript—and you can follow the link or source to confirm.

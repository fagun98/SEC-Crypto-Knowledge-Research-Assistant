2) METHODOLOGY NOTE
- Approach: Read and coded five SEC roundtable meeting reports/transcripts; extracted speaker attributions, policy proposals, explicit staff directives, and operational examples; synthesized into topic taxonomy, cross‑meeting frequency tables and speaker–topic matrices; assessed influence by combining formal authority + evidence indicators (repetition, directives, alignment with staff actions).
- Meetings analyzed: 5 (full list in Sources).
- Data sources: the five provided meeting report files (primary); meeting transcripts contained within those files; cited SEC staff actions noted in meeting text (e.g., SAB 122 rescission) used as external corroboration.
- Confidence scoring: High/Medium/Low based on strength and multiplicity of signals:
  - High = explicit Chair/Commissioner statements or repeated cross‑meeting staff directives + external staff action (e.g., rescission), corroborated by multiple speakers.
  - Medium = multiple mentions across meetings or majority panel support without explicit staff commitment.
  - Low = single‑meeting mentions or proposals by non‑authority actors without corroboration.
- Limitations: speaker word‑counts not computed quantitatively; where transcripts used first names only, affiliations were taken from meeting pages when available. All inferences are labeled.

7) WHAT THIS MEANS FOR...
Action‑oriented implications and priorities (bullet lists)

Issuers
- What to prepare:
  - Document plans for tokenization and custody arrangements: disclose which custodian/transfer agent model you will use; prepare contract templates and technical description of token mechanics (cited as evidence buckets by custody panelists).
  - Assess whether tokenized instruments materially change investor rights — be ready to argue “same security, same treatment” or detail why deviation is needed.
- Immediate actions:
  - Submit written comments and participate in pilots/sandboxes; build pilot use‑cases (transfer agent modernization, tokenized funds).
  - Engage counsel to produce disclosure templates tailored to token economics (per Brummer’s disclosure framing).
- Compliance risk note: expect SEC attention to fundraising/offering stage (Howey prongs), so pre‑issuance legal review remains essential.

DeFi Protocols
- Regulatory considerations:
  - Focus on activity evidence: who sets fees, controls upgrades, holds keys — these operational indicators determine regulatory exposure (DeFi roundtable).
  - Preserve code publication claims but document operational separation between publication and administration; reduce centralization signals (admin keys, centralized order books).
- Immediate actions:
  - Harden governance to reduce central control indicators (timelocks, multisig, clear separation of duties).
  - Prepare activity descriptions, admin key maps, and notices explaining governance/upgrade processes for regulators.
  - Consider participation in pilots that test how activity‑based regulation could apply.
- Compliance risk note: publishing code is not a safe harbor if protocol developers/operators exercise functions that map to intermediated services (EtherDelta example cited in DeFi meeting).

Exchanges / ATSs
- Compliance implications:
  - Expect staff interest in periodic reporting, surveillance/AML cooperation, and ability to integrate tokenized securities onto existing ATS/exchange frameworks.
  - ATS and SPBD models are under exploration — firms should model how tokenized listings will satisfy 15c2‑11, surveillance, and best execution analogs.
- Immediate actions:
  - Evaluate systems for on‑chain/off‑chain order handling, ability to pause/force settlement holds, and cross‑market surveillance.
  - Engage with staff on sandbox participation; prepare proof‑of‑concepts for tokenized trading under conditional exemptive relief.
- Operational note: Market‑structure design choices (atomic settlement vs netting) will influence regulator acceptance — provide quantified risk analyses.

Custodians / Compliance Teams
- Operational impacts:
  - Expect objective “evidence buckets” for proving control (contracts, SOC/crypto audits, key ceremony attestation, on‑chain proofs).
  - Regulators will expect technology‑neutral controls: KYC/AML, segregation, auditability, capital/resolution plans where applicable.
- Immediate actions:
  - Prepare standardized attestation packages (audits, key ceremony records, proof‑of‑control flow charts).
  - Engage in public comments proposing qualified‑custodian criteria; pilot proposals to show bankruptcy remoteness and recovery processes.
  - Ensure readiness to demonstrate staking/delegation controls, reporting on delegation risks.
- Compliance note: preservation of investor protections (SIPC/SIPA gaps) remains unsettled — custodians should prepare contingency procedures and client disclosures.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- Purpose: The SEC Crypto Task Force’s second roundtable examined how crypto assets are traded, what trading platforms and participants should be subject to SEC regulation, how tokenized securities might be treated, and related issues (custody, market structure, surveillance, cross-jurisdictional issues).
- Format: Opening remarks from SEC leadership, two moderated sessions with a multi‑disciplinary panel (industry, exchanges, academics, DeFi), and audience/Q&A. The moderator (Nicholas Losurdo) guided a “what/who/how” taxonomy in Session 1 and a focused “what the SEC can do” session in Session 2.
- Key outcomes: No binding rulemaking announced. The roundtable generated concrete ideas the staff can explore: taxonomy of tokens, disclosure standards (15c2-11 analogs), pathway for tokenized securities (exchange / ATS / special‑purpose broker-dealer models), sandbox/exemptive relief for limited testing, improved regulator coordination (SEC/CFTC), and more resources/data tools for surveillance. Next roundtables (custody, tokenization, DeFi) were announced.
- Central tension: balancing investor protection (and existing securities-market guardrails) with not stifling innovation or misapplying analogies from traditional market structure (e.g., wholesale adoption of Reg NMS models).

3. VIEWS ON TOPICS
Below are the main topics discussed, the viewpoints represented, and where the panel converged or disagreed.

Topic: Scope / Jurisdiction (SEC vs CFTC vs states)
- Viewpoints:
  - Many panelists urged clearer token taxonomy and a single (or coordinated) federal approach to avoid the state license “patchwork” and offshore flight of activity (Austin, Richard).
  - Several participants (Dave, Tyler) emphasized operational overlap and urged close SEC–CFTC cooperation; some (Richard Johnson, at one point) advocated merging SEC and CFTC.
  - DeFi representatives (Katherine) argued peer‑to‑peer/self‑custody activity is fundamentally different and should be outside broker/exchange-style regulation.
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus on need for clarity and better agency coordination. Disagreement about degree of consolidation (merge agencies vs cooperation) and whether peer‑to‑peer should be left outside SEC reach.

Topic: Which intermediaries to regulate (brokers, exchanges, ATS, special purpose broker-dealer)
- Viewpoints:
  - Broad support for focusing on intermediaries that custody or act as brokers first — “start with the broker” (Tyler, Greg, Richard).
  - Some recommended updating/creating a special‑purpose broker‑dealer / SPBD to permit custody of non‑security crypto assets alongside securities (Dave, Greg).
  - ATS model suggested as useful, particularly early-stage (Greg), while others argued for national exchange pathways for tokenized securities where possible (Dave, Jon).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus that intermediaries that custody or execute for retail investors need priority. Disagreement about whether existing broker‑dealer/FINRA/ATS prerequisites should be re‑used or re‑engineered for crypto. Most opposed creating a new, separate SRO.

Topic: Tokenized securities (registration, trading, and the exchange/ATS pathway)
- Viewpoints:
  - Many favored pathways to allow tokenized securities on registered exchanges or under an adapted ATS / SPBD model; desire for workable disclosure/registration standards was strong (Dave, Greg, Richard).
  - Some argued token type matters — tokens that start as investment contracts may evolve; there should be guidance on when secondary trading exits securities status (Chelsea, Katherine).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus that a clear path for tokenized securities is desirable; disagreement about how to treat tokens that evolve from investment contract → decentralized token (some want exit criteria/guidance, others said doctrinal issues make it hard).

Topic: DeFi / peer-to-peer / self‑custody
- Viewpoints:
  - DeFi advocates (Katherine) argued that purely peer‑to‑peer self‑custody should not be regulated as if there were an intermediary because the infrastructure is not an intermediary.
  - Other panelists emphasized investor protection for users who interact through intermediaries; regulators should not assume that all activity is intermediated (Christine, Dave).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus to avoid overbroad regulation that would penalize self‑custody, but disagreement on where to draw the line when DeFi services have intermediating functionality.

Topic: Market structure and legacy rules (Reg NMS, Order Protection Rule, consolidated tape)
- Viewpoints:
  - Strong debate: some argued many NMS features are ill‑suited to crypto (OPR would be problematic) and may have generated negative market outcomes in equities (Greg, Richard).
  - Others argued the functions behind NMS (transparency, consolidated tape, disclosures) are essential and should be re-implemented in ways suitable for crypto (Tyler, Dave).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus for function-over-form approach: keep the useful functions (surveillance, transparency, best execution) but do not blindly transplant all NMS rules. Disagreements on particular rules (OPR) and whether competition vs regulation would have achieved the same results historically.

Topic: Surveillance, data, and on‑chain analytics
- Viewpoints:
  - Panelists widely urged improved data collection and analytic capabilities for regulators (Mark Uyeda, Dave). Public chains offer new data sources but have limits (wallet anonymity, off‑chain trades).
  - Some emphasized the utility of a consolidated tape / MIDAS-style systems, adapted to on‑ and off‑chain data (Tyler).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus that regulators must dramatically upgrade data/analytic capabilities; disagreement on how to balance privacy and granular surveillance.

Topic: Disclosure and listings (15c2-11 analogs, registration criteria)
- Viewpoints:
  - Practical proposals to adapt 15c2-11/15c3-3 rules to crypto (Chelsea, Richard, Dave); recommend clearer issuer/disclosure obligations for trading platforms and bright-line tests for secondary‑market treatment.
  - Some proposed Commissioner Peirce-like Rule 195-style frameworks for initial offering disclosures and an “exit report” for decentralization.
- Consensus/Disagreements: Consensus on need for standardized disclosure frameworks to enable market participants and protect investors; debate on exact content (financial statements vs other chain-native info).

Topic: Settlement / Clearing / Staking / ETFs (creates/redeems and in-kind)
- Viewpoints:
  - Many panelists supported allowing in‑kind creates/redeems for crypto ETPs and thought staking could be accommodated with careful liquidity analysis (Greg, Jon, Chelsea).
  - Panelists noted tradeoffs between atomic on‑chain settlement and the benefits of central clearing/netting (Jon).
- Consensus/Disagreements: Agreement that settlement innovations are promising but require careful design; disagreement on immediacy and operational readiness for large-scale on-chain settlement.

4. MEETING CONCLUSION
- Overall outcome: The roundtable produced a set of concrete themes and possible policy levers for the SEC to explore, but no immediate rulemaking. The Commission emphasized collecting public input and continuing the roundtable series (custody, tokenization, DeFi are the next topics).
- Action items / next steps (implicit or explicitly proposed):
  - Continue public engagement; staff to read >100 written submissions already filed (transcript).
  - Explore taxonomy and clear regulatory assignments for token types.
  - Consider disclosure frameworks (15c2-11 analog / Rule 195-like ideas) for issuers and platforms.
  - Consider special‑purpose broker‑dealer and ATS/adapted exchange pathways (plus sandbox/exemptive relief) to enable tokenized securities and side‑by‑side trading.
  - Improve cross‑agency (SEC/CFTC) coordination; consider whether deeper institutional change is needed (some speakers suggested merger; others argued for pragmatic cooperation).
  - Enhance regulator data/analytic capabilities to monitor on‑ and off‑chain activity.
  - Pursue follow‑up roundtables and publish learnings (custody roundtable scheduled in two weeks; tokenization in May; DeFi in June - transcript closing).
- Key takeaways:
  - All sides want clarity. Industry wants legal certainty to build markets and bring trading/custody onshore. Regulators and academics emphasize investor protection and market integrity. DeFi advocates ask that self‑custody and peer‑to‑peer flows not be automatically treated as intermediated activity.

5. KEY POINTS AND MAIN ARGUMENTS/EVENTS
Major points and highly‑cited lines (direct quotes from transcript):
- Opening framing:
  - Richard Gabbert (Crypto Task Force): roundtable is part of “spring sprint toward crypto clarity” (transcript).
  - Acting Chair Mark Uyeda: “...the drafters of the federal securities laws did not contemplate the use of blockchains or smart contracts...a time-limited conditional exemptive relief framework...could allow for greater innovation within the United States.” (transcript)
- Cross‑cutting themes:
  - Investor protection remains primary: “there's nothing magical about the blockchain that eliminates the core principles of investor protection.” — Dave Lauer (transcript)
  - Need for clarity/taxonomy: multiple panelists asked for a clear token taxonomy and jurisdictional clarity to prevent regulatory patchwork and offshore migration (Austin, Richard, Dave).
  - Tradeoffs between innovation and existing structure: several panelists encouraged re‑examining legacy rules (e.g., Reg NMS concepts) but urged keeping critical functions (surveillance, best‑execution transparency) (Tyler, Greg, Dave).
- Specific arguments / suggested policy tools:
  - Special‑purpose broker‑dealer and ATS adaptation: pattern‑matching to broker‑dealer/ATS to allow custody/trading under known guardrails (Greg, Dave).
  - Disclosure adaptation (15c2-11 / Rule 195 / exit reports): create bright lines for issuer disclosures and for when a token “exits” securities treatment (Chelsea, Katherine, Richard).
  - Sandboxes & exemptive relief: time‑limited market tests under SEC oversight to experiment with ledger-based settlement and side‑by‑side trading (Mark Uyeda suggestion; panel supported the idea).
  - Surveillance/data modernization: build capacity to ingest on‑chain and off‑chain data; regulators need modern analytics (Dave, Tyler).
  - Global considerations: reciprocity and competitiveness to get trading/custody back onshore (Austin).
- Notable exchanges / moments:
  - Robust debate about whether to import NMS rules wholesale to crypto — many panelists argued for keeping functional goals (transparency, competition) but not mechanical rule transplants (OPR controversy) (Greg, Richard, Tyler).
  - Broad support for “start with the broker” principle (Tyler), prioritizing regulation where retail investors interface with intermediaries.
  - DeFi voice (Katherine) strongly advocated not to treat the underlying peer‑to‑peer infrastructure as an “intermediary” where no custody or centralized control exists.

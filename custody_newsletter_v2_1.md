## 🔥 Top Stories

1. **SEC Cyber Exam Priorities (FY 2026) emphasize controls that directly underpin crypto custody** (Feb. 9, 2026 — SEC Cybersecurity page)  
   Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity

   **FACTS (from SEC.gov)**
   - The SEC cybersecurity page states: “During fiscal year 2026, exam priorities of the SEC’s Division of Examinations include registrants’ policies and procedures, governance practices, data loss prevention, access controls, account management, and responses to cyber-related incidents such as ransomware attacks.”  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity
   - It also states the Division “will review controls to mitigate new risks associated with artificial intelligence (AI) and polymorphic malware attacks.”  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity
   - The page links investor education materials including: “Investor Bulletin: Crypto Asset Custody Basics for Retail Investors.”  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/cybersecurity (see “Investor Information” section)

   **ANALYSIS**
   - Even though this is framed as “cyber,” the listed priorities are **core custody controls** in practice for crypto: private key security, privileged access management, segregation of duties, secure recovery procedures, and incident response.
   - The explicit reference to AI and polymorphic malware increases the bar for **continuous monitoring, anomaly detection, and resilient operational processes** (not just static policies).

   **INFERENCE**
   - Expect examinations to request **evidence** (not just written policies): access reviews, key-ceremony records, device hardening baselines, incident tabletop documentation, and vendor/third-party security artifacts for custody platforms.

   **Implications**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - Prioritize demonstrable controls around **account management and access controls** (e.g., admin rights, break-glass access, logging).  
     - Prepare for exam scrutiny that ties cyber controls to **customer protection** expectations (even when crypto assets are involved).
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - Strengthen oversight of custody vendors (SOC reports, penetration testing summaries, key management attestations) and align incident response playbooks with client communications.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - Expect examiner interest in how traditional bank control frameworks apply to **wallet infrastructure**, HSM configurations, and outsourced custody technology stacks.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - Treat FY 2026 exam priorities as a practical checklist: governance, data loss prevention, access controls, and ransomware readiness—mapped directly to key custody workflows (deposit/withdrawal, key rotation, recovery).

2. **SEC division pages (Trading & Markets; Investment Management) reinforce where custody oversight sits—useful for engagement and escalation paths** (Feb. 9, 2026 — SEC Division pages)  
   Sources:  
   - https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets  
   - https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-investment-management

   **FACTS (from SEC.gov)**
   - Trading & Markets states it “regulates the major securities market participants, including broker-dealers, self-regulatory organizations, and transfer agents.”  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets
   - Investment Management states it “oversees investment advisers and investment companies” and “advises the Commission on whether to propose, adopt, or amend rules and forms under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and the Investment Company Act of 1940.”  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-investment-management
   - Separately (standing resource), the SEC’s “Crypto Points of Contact” page directs custody questions by division (IM for adviser custody questions; TM for broker-dealer custody).  
     Source: https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force/crypto-points-contact-secgov

   **ANALYSIS**
   - This is not new rule text, but it matters operationally: custody-related questions—and staff interpretations—often turn on **the registrant type** (BD vs RIA vs fund vs transfer agent).
   - For crypto custody programs spanning multiple regulated entities, these pages support building an internal “regulatory routing map” for questions, disclosures, exams, and incident reporting.

   **INFERENCE**
   - As tokenized securities and broker-dealer custody frameworks evolve, firms should expect **continued split-division ownership** (TM for Exchange Act financial responsibility/custody; IM for Advisers Act custody/qualified custodian topics), requiring coordinated compliance responses.

   **Implications**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - Route “possession/control,” customer protection, and financial responsibility questions primarily through **Trading & Markets** channels; maintain an evidence package aligned to TM expectations.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - Keep “qualified custodian” and custody-rule interpretive issues anchored with **Investment Management** frameworks and staff materials.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - If serving both BDs and RIAs, anticipate dual expectations: BD-facing (TM) and adviser-facing (IM).
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - When building institutional offerings, align product documentation to the **client’s regulator** (e.g., RIA custody diligence vs BD possession/control).

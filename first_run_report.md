- It addresses **regulatory guidance on which entities may hold crypto**.
- It identifies **paths to registration for custodial market intermediaries**.
- It notes **policy coordination affecting custody standards**.
  **Citation:** SEC Crypto Task Force featured topic page.¹

- It addresses **operational rules affecting who can hold customer crypto and custody arrangements**.
  **Citation:** SEC Division of Trading and Markets page.²

### C. SEC Division of Investment Management — adviser/fund custody focus

- Investment Management covers **custody rules under the Advisers Act and Investment Company Act as applied to crypto**.
- It addresses **qualified custodians and custody requirements for funds holding crypto**.
- It discusses **segregation and asset safekeeping for fund and adviser custody**.
- It references **exemptive relief and orders related to custody arrangements**.
  **Citation:** SEC Division of Investment Management page.³

## 2) ANALYSIS (what these facts mean for crypto custody programs)

### Theme 1 — “Who is allowed to custody” remains a central regulatory question

The SEC’s framing (Crypto Task Force + Trading & Markets + Investment Management) reinforces that custody is not just an operational choice—**it is tied to entity status (BD, RIA, fund custodian, clearing agency), registration pathways, and customer protection rules**.

### Theme 2 — Expect custody requirements to be enforced through multiple “lanes”

The SEC’s custody posture shows three distinct but overlapping lanes:

- **Broker-dealer lane (Trading & Markets):** customer protection, possession/control, segregation, operational readiness, and any staff interpretations.
- **Adviser/fund lane (Investment Management):** qualified custodian expectations, safekeeping, and custody rule compliance outcomes.
- **Cross-cutting lane (Crypto Task Force):** coordination and alignment on standards and “paths to registration” for custody-related intermediaries.

### Theme 3 — “Segregation and safekeeping” is a shared control objective across regimes

Both Divisions explicitly point to **segregation/safekeeping**, suggesting that—regardless of structure—firms should be able to demonstrate:

- clear segregation of client assets,
- clear books/records support for who owns what,
- operational controls that support those outcomes.

---

## 3) INFERENCE (reasonable implications; not stated in the sources)

- **Increased scrutiny of custody business models:** If the SEC is highlighting “which entities may hold crypto” and “paths to registration,” firms should assume that custody structures that rely on informal or unclear status (e.g., “we’re not a custodian”) may face challenge.
- **More reliance on staff positions:** Because Trading & Markets references no-action/interpretive guidance, firms should assume that **staff interpretation** may materially affect permissible custody designs even absent new rules.
- **Convergence on control expectations:** Even if legal regimes differ (BD vs RIA vs fund), regulators may converge on common evidence: segregation, safekeeping, operational resiliency, and auditability.

# Role-Based Impact (plain English)

## Broker-Dealer (BD)

**Impact**

- Custody design will be evaluated through **BD custody rules**, **segregation**, and **operational constraints** on holding customer crypto asset securities.²
- You may need to justify whether your crypto activity is custody, clearing, or an intermediary function, and whether a registration pathway applies.¹

**Practical pressure points**

- How you demonstrate customer asset segregation (on-chain + off-chain records).
- Whether your arrangements look like you’re acting as an unregistered custodian/clearing function.
- Readiness to adapt if staff guidance shifts.

---

## Registered Investment Adviser (RIA)

**Impact**

- Custody obligations under the Advisers Act (as applied to crypto) and the concept of **qualified custodians** remain central.³
- If you use third-party crypto custodians, your due diligence and oversight will be tested against safekeeping and segregation expectations.³

**Practical pressure points**

- Whether each crypto venue/custodian used meets “qualified custodian” expectations for your facts.
- Whether wallet/control structures accidentally create adviser “custody” (even if unintended).
- Audit trail quality and client reporting.

---

## Bank (including trust bank [/](https://file+.vscode-resource.vscode-cdn.net/) bank custody business)

**Impact**

- The SEC framing emphasizes “which entities may hold crypto” and custody standards coordination.¹
- Banks acting as custodians for SEC-regulated clients should expect counterparties (BDs/RIAs/funds) to demand stronger proof of segregation, safekeeping, and operational controls aligned to SEC expectations.¹³

**Practical pressure points**

- Providing evidence packages (controls, segregation attestations, incident response, governance).
- Contract terms around sub-custody, wallet control, insolvency protections, and books/records.

---

## Custodian [/](https://file+.vscode-resource.vscode-cdn.net/) Crypto Custodian (specialist, trust company, or market intermediary)

**Impact**

- The SEC explicitly signals **paths to registration for custodial market intermediaries** and policy coordination affecting custody standards.¹
- Your product design may be assessed not only as “tech custody” but as regulated custody/market intermediation depending on services offered (settlement, clearing-like functions, rehypothecation, staking, etc.).¹²

**Practical pressure points**

- Clarity of your regulatory perimeter and disclosures: what you do (and do not do).
- Demonstrable segregation and safekeeping controls.
- Ability to support BD and RIA compliance obligations with reporting and controls evidence.

# Compliance Actions (do-this-now checklist)

## For Broker-Dealers

1. **Document custody classification:** For each crypto product/service, document whether you hold customer assets, control keys, or facilitate settlement (tie to “custody of crypto asset securities”).¹²
2. **Segregation evidence pack:** Maintain reconciliations (on-chain balances ↔ internal ledger ↔ customer statements), plus documented wallet governance and approval workflows.²
3. **Interpretation monitoring:** Create a defined process to intake and implement SEC staff interpretive/no-action developments (who reviews, how fast policies update).²

## For RIAs

1. **Custody rule mapping for crypto:** For each strategy, identify whether adviser “custody” is triggered (authority, wallets, ability to move assets) and what controls mitigate risk.³
2. **Qualified custodian due diligence refresh:** Reconfirm that each crypto custodian/venue used supports your custody compliance (safekeeping, segregation, reporting).³
3. **Client disclosure alignment:** Ensure disclosures match the actual custody chain (who holds keys, where assets sit, what happens on insolvency).

## For Banks [/](https://file+.vscode-resource.vscode-cdn.net/) Trust Banks

1. **Client-facing controls package:** Prepare standardized artifacts for SEC-regulated clients: segregation approach, control governance, audit/SOC materials, incident response overview.¹³
2. **Contract and sub-custody clarity:** Tighten provisions on sub-custody, key control, rehypothecation (if any), and insolvency treatment to support “safekeeping” expectations.³

## For Crypto Custodians [/](https://file+.vscode-resource.vscode-cdn.net/) Market Intermediaries

1. **Regulatory perimeter memo:** Clearly define whether your services could be viewed as custody, brokerage, clearing-like, or exchange-like functions and what “path to registration” might apply.¹²
2. **Operational proof of segregation:** Be able to show segregation technically (wallet architecture) and administratively (books/records, reconciliations, access controls).²³
3. **Support your clients’ compliance:** Provide downloadable reconciliation reports, audit logs, and statements that BDs/RIAs can use to satisfy their own regulatory obligations.²³

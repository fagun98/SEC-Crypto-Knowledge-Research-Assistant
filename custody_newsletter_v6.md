## The Regulatory Digest for Crypto Custodians

## 🔥 Top Stories

1. **Project Crypto + SEC-CFTC harmonization + SEC Crypto Task Force (Feb 3–4, 2026 | SEC Chair/Featured Topics)**
   - **FACTS (what was said/announced)**
     - Chairman Paul S. Atkins said the SEC will pursue “Project Crypto” jointly with the CFTC to explore tokenization and an “innovation exemption framework” for **permissioned on-chain finance**, while “preserv[ing] investor protection, transparency, and supervisory oversight.” (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-remarks-afme-020426)
     - The SEC Crypto Task Force states it will work to clarify how federal securities laws apply to crypto assets, including drawing lines between securities and non-securities, crafting tailored disclosure frameworks, and providing “realistic paths to registration” for crypto assets and intermediaries; it will gather input via meetings, roundtables, written submissions, and information requests. (https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)
     - The SEC-CFTC Harmonization Initiative describes a joint SEC/CFTC effort to reduce duplicative/conflicting requirements, clarify jurisdictional boundaries, and create streamlined pathways for innovative products/services, including through stakeholder engagement and written input. (https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)
   - **ANALYSIS (custody takeaways)**
     - The combined signal is that **the “path to market” for tokenized securities and permissioned on-chain infrastructure is likely to run through (a) engagement, (b) scoped exemptions/pilots, and (c) demonstrable custody-grade controls** (segregation, key management, auditability, and supervisory oversight) rather than broad deregulation.
     - Expect custody expectations to be expressed first through **conditions in exemptions/no-action positions** and **pilot guardrails**, not through a single new “crypto custody rule” in the near term.
   - **INFERENCE (what to prepare for next)**
     - Firms that can document end-to-end custody controls (wallet governance, reconciliations, access logs, incident response, independent assurance) will be best positioned to participate in any “permissioned on-chain” frameworks without getting stalled in interpretive uncertainty.

   **Implications**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** Project Crypto is framed as SEC+CFTC work on tokenization and an innovation exemption framework for permissioned on-chain finance, with investor protection and supervisory oversight preserved. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-remarks-afme-020426)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Start mapping how BD customer protection controls (segregation, recordkeeping, supervision) would be evidenced on-chain (permissions, attestations, surveillance) before pilots open.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - **FACTS:** The Crypto Task Force says it aims to craft tailored disclosure frameworks and registration paths and will solicit written input/roundtables. (https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)
     - **ANALYSIS:** RIAs should anticipate sharper questions in exams around *how* they diligence custodians supporting tokenized assets (keys, sub-custody, insolvency treatment, audit coverage).
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - **FACTS:** The Harmonization Initiative’s stated goal includes eliminating duplicative/conflicting requirements and clarifying SEC/CFTC jurisdictional boundaries through coordinated engagement. (https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/sec-cftc-harmonization-initiative)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Banks/trusts can position themselves as “control points” in permissioned systems—if they can evidence safekeeping, segregation, and audit-ready reporting across on-chain/off-chain rails.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** The Task Force explicitly contemplates “realistic paths to registration” for “market intermediaries” and plans public engagement to shape policy. (https://www.sec.gov/featured-topics/crypto-task-force)
     - **INFERENCE:** Expect “registration-path” conversations to quickly become “custody-control” conversations—SOC coverage, governance, and provable segregation will be gating items.

2. **SEC Investment Management signals “pilot/no-action first” approach to AI (Feb 3, 2026 | IM Division Director remarks)**
   - **FACTS**
     - Brian Daly (Director, Division of Investment Management) said AI adoption is uneven and urged engagement with the Division; he discouraged rushing to prescriptive rules that could become obsolete and invited firms to propose pilots, request no-action letters, or seek staff guidance. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
     - Daly highlighted regulatory issues AI raises, including recordkeeping, supervision, marketing, and fiduciary questions. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
     - Daly floated the concept of reimagining investor disclosure using large language models (e.g., fund-provided AI agents trained on prospectuses). (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
   - **ANALYSIS (custody angle)**
     - For crypto custody programs, AI is not just a “front office” issue—AI increasingly touches **reconciliations, exception management, client reporting, and incident response**, which are core custody controls. If AI is used, firms should treat model outputs as regulated records and supervise them like any other client communication.
   - **INFERENCE**
     - We expect near-term SEC staff attention to focus on whether AI use **weakens** (or can be shown to strengthen) control evidence: audit trails, reproducibility, approvals, and escalation paths.

   **Implications**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** Daly flagged recordkeeping and supervision as key issues raised by AI use. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
     - **ANALYSIS:** If you deploy AI in ops (tickets, reconciliations, client comms), ensure outputs are retained, attributable, and reviewable under your supervision framework.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - **FACTS:** The Division invited pilots and requests for no-action/staff guidance for novel AI uses. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Funds/advisers using AI disclosure agents should align custody disclosures (who holds keys/assets; what’s segregated) with AI-provided answers to avoid inconsistent investor communications.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - **ANALYSIS:** Treat AI governance as operational risk management for custody: access controls to sensitive custody data, change management, and evidence of human oversight for exceptions.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** Daly acknowledged liability/supervision challenges and emphasized engagement rather than immediate prescriptive rules. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/daly-020326-artificial-intelligence-future-investment-management)
     - **INFERENCE:** Expect counterparties (fund admins, auditors, prime brokers) to ask whether AI-assisted processes preserve “four-eyes” controls and immutable logs.

3. **Tokenization + Treasury clearing infrastructure updates (Feb 9, 2026 | Commissioner Uyeda remarks)**
   - **FACTS**
     - Commissioner Mark Uyeda discussed Treasury market reforms and noted the SEC approved registration of **CME Securities Clearing Inc.** and **ICE Clear Credit LLC** as clearing agencies for U.S. Treasury securities. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - Uyeda said the SEC approved FICC rule changes including a **collateral-in-lieu** service and expanding **agent clearing** to include triparty transactions; a proposed FICC–CME cross-margining customer expansion remains under review and includes a petition for exemptive relief under Exchange Act provisions. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - On tokenization, Uyeda described a technology-neutral approach and indicated willingness to use sub-regulatory guidance and exemptive relief to permit limited pilots while ensuring “custody, disclosure, and investor protections” are met. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
   - **ANALYSIS (custody angle)**
     - Tokenized Treasuries (and tokenized fund interests holding Treasuries) will likely be evaluated against the same core custody outcomes: **clear ownership records, segregation, reliable settlement finality, and auditable controls**—even if the asset rail is “on-chain.”
     - Clearing model changes (cross-margining, collateral-in-lieu) can create operational complexity for custodians supporting broker-dealers and funds, particularly around **collateral movements, lien/perfection concepts, and reconciliations**.
   - **INFERENCE**
     - If tokenization pilots move forward via exemptive relief, expect conditions that require (a) custody control evidence, (b) third-party assurance, and (c) clear disclosures about how on-chain entitlements map to off-chain legal rights.

   **Implications**
   - **Broker-dealers & crypto custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** Uyeda noted FICC’s collateral-in-lieu and agent-clearing triparty expansions and that a FICC–CME cross-margining customer expansion is under review. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Broker-dealers should assess how these clearing/collateral mechanics interact with customer protection frameworks (segregation, reserve methodologies, and operational control evidence) as settlement models evolve.
   - **RIAs & investment funds:**
     - **FACTS:** Uyeda referenced tokenization pilots using exemptive relief while maintaining custody/disclosure/investor-protection guardrails. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Funds considering tokenized share classes should pre-plan the qualified-custody story: where assets sit, how they’re reconciled, and what investors can verify.
   - **Banks/trust companies:**
     - **FACTS:** Uyeda described ongoing SEC engagement on the inter-affiliate exemption and extraterritorial scope questions for Treasury clearing implementation. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - **ANALYSIS:** Banks/trusts supporting cross-border groups should prepare for “scope + control” mapping—entity-by-entity operational responsibility, booking models, and custody control points.
   - **Crypto-native custodians:**
     - **FACTS:** Uyeda indicated tokenized securities must still meet custody, disclosure, and investor protection expectations, with exemptive relief and guidance enabling limited pilots. (https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-remarks-asset-management-derivatives-forum-020926)
     - **INFERENCE:** Crypto custodians seeking to custody tokenized Treasuries/securities should expect to integrate with traditional clearing/custody expectations (audits, legal opinions on entitlements, and institutional-grade controls).
